## 19. Loss and frequency-band diagnosis after listening (2026-07-16)

The user rejected multi-checkpoint listening because the perceptual differences are subtle and listening to a dense transition series would be expensive and unreliable. The next route is loss-level diagnosis.

The 30k run logged one sampled training batch every 100 steps. The weighted objective is dominated by spectral terms. Median reconstructed contribution by 5k-step phase changed approximately as follows:

```text
steps 1-5k:    spectral 97.3%, feature matching 1.5%, adversarial -0.1%, KL 1.3%
steps 25-30k:  spectral 85.2%, feature matching 8.6%, adversarial 5.1%, KL 1.1%
```

Important implementation details:

- time-domain L1 weight is exactly zero;
- the official stereo loss module list contains the same sum/difference `mrstft_loss` twice, plus half-weight left and right STFT terms;
- scalar loss contribution is not equivalent to optimization influence, because gradient norms and gradient directions differ;
- logs contain training batches only, not a fixed held-out validation set, so the rising total loss cannot be interpreted as worsening generalization.

A direct frequency-band comparison against each normalized original was then run on the three listening samples. The trained Decoders generally reduce mean absolute log-magnitude error, but also reduce high-frequency energy relative to the source. Depending on sample and Decoder, attenuation is roughly 0.3-1.6 dB above 4 kHz. On sample04, 8-12 kHz energy changes from -0.71 dB for the original Decoder to -1.11 dB online and -1.30 dB EMA. This quantitatively supports the user's suspected loss of high-frequency detail.

## 20. Fixed-test loss-gradient attribution (2026-07-16)

A new isolated experiment was completed without training or modifying official vendor code. Three held-out sources were each sampled at four deterministic windows. Original, online-30k, and EMA-30k Decoders shared the same original-Encoder latent. The final discriminator was frozen. Official spectral, feature-matching, and adversarial losses plus a diagnostic 4-20 kHz log-magnitude loss were evaluated, including gradients with respect to both Decoder parameters and output waveform.

Results: online and EMA reduce median log-magnitude error at all seven inspected FFT sizes, confirming the reconstruction improvement. They also attenuate 8-20 kHz more than the original Decoder. No single adversarial term is proven to be the main cause: GAN gradients are often weakly opposed to the high-frequency diagnostic, but cosine magnitude is near zero and unstable. Feature matching is weakly aligned but also near zero. The official spectral objective is positively aligned but near zero, meaning it improves average spectrum without strongly protecting high-frequency detail.

The likely structural cause is that official MR-STFT averages seven resolutions, uses A-weighting, duplicates sum/difference MR-STFT in stereo, and has no explicit high-frequency-retention term or time-domain L1. The evidence supports a minimal controlled continuation experiment: equal-step control versus the same run with a small 4-20 kHz log-magnitude auxiliary loss. Do not change GAN or feature-matching weights first.
